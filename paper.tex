\title{Mechanisms and Limits of Sycophancy Mitigation\\in Instruction-Tuned LLMs}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

\maketitle


\begin{abstract}
We apply controlled probes, activation patching, head ablation, and preference-based fine-tuning to sycophancy in instruction-tuned LLMs. On Llama-3-8B-Instruct, DPO fine-tuning across three independent training seeds reduces opinion sycophancy 82.4\% $\to$ 57.1\,$\pm$\,2.8\% while preserving full-$N{=}1{,}319$ GSM8k (40.2\,$\pm$\,2.9\% vs.\ 33.2\% baseline); SFT on identical preference data collapses GSM8k to 5.8\%. Format-controlled probes (neutral-transfer design) on Llama-3 and Mistral-7B suggest the dominant pre-DPO failure mode under our forced-choice evaluation is \textbf{social compliance}---correct-answer information remains linearly decodable from residual-stream activations under biased prompts while output selection favors the user-aligned answer (18.0\% vs.\ 10.1\% belief corruption in Llama-3, Fisher's $p<0.001$); post-DPO the decomposition shifts toward robust tracking ($-$6.6\,pp / $+$15.6\,pp). Activation patching identifies attention heads that carry the sycophantic signal, but ablating the top 10 heads simultaneously yields no reduction ($+$0.5\,pp Llama-3, $+$1.0\,pp Mistral)---a \textbf{patching-to-ablation dissociation}: the heads are sufficient carriers, not causally necessary. A 5-resample bootstrap shows the head ranking is unstable (top-3 pairwise Jaccard 0.09) yet the ablation null holds for every resampled head set. Generality is bounded: Mistral DPO collapses GSM8k under matched preference-pair construction; Qwen-14B exhibits a near-zero compliance gap; free-form evaluation directionally supports the findings without statistical significance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sycophancy---a language model's tendency to validate user beliefs regardless of factual accuracy---is a documented failure mode of RLHF-trained models \citep{perez2022discovering, sharma2024towards} that undermines their usefulness for honest information retrieval, reasoning, and advice. Two competing internal mechanisms could produce this behavior: \textbf{belief corruption} (the biased social context reduces the decodability of the correct-answer representation; residual-stream activations now encode the sycophantic answer) or \textbf{social compliance} (the correct-answer signal remains decodable from residual-stream activations, but output selection favors the user-aligned answer). The two predict different mitigations---knowledge-representation interventions for the former, output-gating interventions for the latter.

A separate methodological question concerns mechanistic interpretability itself. Activation patching \citep{wang2022interpretability} identifies which components \emph{can carry} a behavioral signal under intervention, but whether those components are causally \emph{necessary} requires ablation \citep{heimersheim2024patching}. For complex behaviors implemented through redundant pathways, patching-identified components may be ablated with no measurable effect---a sufficiency-vs-necessity dissociation with direct consequences for which interventions can be expected to work.

We apply controlled probes, activation patching, head ablation, and preference-based fine-tuning to sycophancy in Llama-3-8B-Instruct, with cross-architecture replication on Mistral-7B-Instruct and cross-scale evaluation on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Controlled probes (neutral-transfer design with randomized answer positions) suggest social compliance dominates belief corruption in Llama-3 (1.8:1) and Mistral-7B (6.3:1) under our forced-choice evaluation.
  \item Activation patching identifies heads that carry the sycophantic signal, but top-3 and top-10 zero-ablations produce no sycophancy reduction in either Llama-3 or Mistral. A 5-resample bootstrap confirms the null is robust to head-selection instability (top-3 pairwise Jaccard 0.09; the ablation null holds for every resampled head set)---an empirical instance of the sufficiency-vs-necessity gap \citep{heimersheim2024patching} consistent with redundant implementation \citep{mcgrath2023hydra, rushing2024explorations}.
  \item In Llama-3, DPO reduces opinion sycophancy across three training seeds (82.4\% $\to$ 57.1\,$\pm$\,2.8\%) with better capability retention than SFT on identical preference data; the probe decomposition shifts from social compliance toward robust tracking.
  \item Cross-model and free-form evaluations bound the claim: Mistral DPO fails to preserve capabilities; Qwen-14B's bias-induced shift is near zero despite high baseline opinion-agreement; free-form gains are directional but not significant at the pilot $N$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.} (a) Probes establish that the model ``believes'' the correct answer in any strong sense; the probe results identify representations correlated with the correct answer that exist independent of prompt-format cues. (b) The social-compliance characterization is universal: Qwen-14B's near-zero compliance gap (\S\ref{sec:generality}) suggests it does not hold at this scale or for this RLHF regime. (c) DPO mitigation transfers across architectures: Mistral collapses under matched construction. (d) Head-level zero-ablation is a viable inference-time intervention; our null result argues against this for sycophancy specifically.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Sycophancy.} \citet{perez2022discovering} introduced the \texttt{anthropic\_opinion} dataset used here; \citet{sharma2024towards} characterized sycophancy as scaling with model capability and being amplified by RLHF; \citet{wei2023simple} demonstrated training-data mitigation via synthetic disagreement. Concurrent mechanistic work on sycophancy \citep{chen2024pinpoint, li2025truth, obrien2026fewbad} is summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:concurrent}.

\paragraph{Mechanistic interpretability.} We adapt activation patching for circuit discovery \citep{wang2022interpretability, conmy2023towards}. \citet{heimersheim2024patching} formalize the distinction between denoising patching (sufficiency) and noising patching (necessity) that motivates our ablation analysis. For probe-based truth representations we build on \citet{marks2023geometry, burns2023discovering}. Self-repair phenomena \citep{mcgrath2023hydra, rushing2024explorations} predict that ablating circuit components may not impair behavior; our patching-to-ablation dissociation is an empirical instance.

\paragraph{Inference-time intervention and preference optimization.} Activation steering \citep{li2023inference, turner2023activation, zou2023representation, panickssery2023steering} adds direction vectors to the residual stream; our results suggest these face the redundancy problem we document. DPO \citep{rafailov2023direct} provides our training-time intervention. Mechanistic analysis of DPO has been applied to toxicity \citep{lee2024mechanistic, yang2024dpo}; we extend this paradigm to sycophancy.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Dataset.} A 1{,}500-sample evaluation set across three domains ($N{=}500$ each): \texttt{anthropic\_opinion} (opinion questions with user-stated positions), \texttt{truthfulqa\_factual} (factual questions where a biased prompt asserts a false belief), and \texttt{gsm8k\_reasoning} (math problems with an incorrect suggested answer). Each sample contains a neutral and a biased prompt. \emph{Sycophancy rate} is the fraction of samples where $P(\text{sycophantic}) > P(\text{correct})$ under the biased prompt; \emph{compliance gap} is $P(\text{sycophantic} \mid \text{biased}) - P(\text{sycophantic} \mid \text{neutral})$. Probes assign each sample to robust tracking (probe correct, output correct), social compliance (probe correct, output sycophantic), belief corruption (probe corrupted, output sycophantic), or other.

\paragraph{Models, probes, patching.} Llama-3-8B-Instruct (primary), Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 (replication), Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (cross-scale), via TransformerLens with \texttt{dtype=float16}. Logistic regression probes are trained at each residual stream layer using a \textbf{neutral-transfer} design (trained on neutral activations, evaluated on biased) to prevent learning prompt-format cues; 5-fold stratified cross-validation on 1{,}500 samples with randomized answer positions. We refer to ``best-transfer layer'' as the layer maximizing biased-condition accuracy under this protocol; Layer 1 in Table~\ref{tab:probe} is selected post-hoc from the per-layer sweep (Appendix~\ref{app:probe_layers}, Figure~\ref{fig:probe_accuracy}) on the same evaluation set, not as a pre-registered choice. We report Layer 1 because it minimises the neutral$\to$biased accuracy drop (11.1\,pp); qualitatively similar (smaller) effects hold at adjacent layers. Following \citet{wang2022interpretability}, we identify critical layers via layer$\times$position patching, then perform head-level patching within the top-5 layers. Zero-ablation tests causal necessity. A 5-resample bootstrap ($N{=}100$ each, seeds 42/123/456/789/1011) quantifies head-ranking stability over critical layers 0--11.

\paragraph{DPO and SFT.} 400 opinion-domain preference pairs constructed from Anthropic-MWE held-out from evaluation; LoRA fine-tuning ($r{=}16$, q/k/v/o); DPO ($\beta{=}0.1$, lr $5{\times}10^{-5}$, 3 epochs); SFT trained on the same chosen responses with identical LoRA configuration and dataset. Three DPO training seeds (100/200/300) regenerate the preference data per seed. Full hyperparameters and training-size sensitivity are in Appendices~\ref{app:reproducibility}--\ref{app:size_sensitivity}.

\subsection{Patching-to-ablation dissociation}
\label{sec:circuit}

Layer$\times$position patching localizes sycophancy carriers to early layers (1--5; mean total effect $2.105 \pm 2.728$ over 34/100 samples with measurable effects; Figure~\ref{fig:dissociation}a); head-level patching identifies L4H28 (recovery $0.443 \pm 0.550$), L4H5 ($0.302 \pm 0.537$), L5H31 ($0.256 \pm 0.549$). Standard deviations exceed means due to right-skewed recovery and small effective sample size; \emph{the specific ranking should be treated as approximate}. A 5-resample bootstrap quantifies this: pairwise Jaccard for the top-3 ranking is 0.09 (range 0.00--0.50); the originally-named top-3 heads appear in only 1/5 resamples each; new heads (L11H24, L2H5, L9H28, L9H0) emerge at 2/5 (Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap}).

Zero-ablation of the validated top-3 heads (individually, in pairs, jointly) produces $\pm 0.3$\,pp change from baseline, well within sampling noise. Extending to all 10 patching-identified heads yields the same null: $+0.5$\,pp ($z{=}0.28$, $p{=}0.78$, 95\% CI $[-2.9, +3.9]$\,pp; Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Patching identifies sufficient carriers, not necessary components: the heads \emph{can} carry the sycophantic signal (patching through them restores honest behavior) but are not causally required. Crucially, the head ranking is unstable but the ablation null is not---it holds for every one of the 5 bootstrap-resampled top-3 head sets, strengthening the dissociation interpretation. Our ablation results are consistent with redundant implementation \citep{mcgrath2023hydra, rushing2024explorations}; the bootstrap-resample evidence is also consistent with a degenerate circuit with multiple redundant pathways. Representation steering experiments (Appendix~\ref{app:steering}) yield only small opinion-domain reductions ($-6.9$\,pp at layer 15, $\alpha{=}2$) that do not survive FDR correction; under aggressive $\alpha$, the intervention destroys capability rather than reducing sycophancy. Together, these inference-time results motivate the training-time intervention in Section~\ref{sec:dpo}.

Across three independent DPO training seeds with regenerated preference data per seed, opinion sycophancy on Llama-3-8B-Instruct drops from 82.4\% to 57.1\,$\pm$\,2.8\% (CV $<$ 12\%); MMLU is preserved at 62.9\,$\pm$\,0.1\% and full-$N{=}1{,}319$ GSM8k at 40.2\,$\pm$\,2.9\% (vs.\ 33.2\% baseline). \textbf{In Llama-3, DPO shifts the probe decomposition from social compliance toward robust tracking}: at Layer 1, social compliance drops $-6.6$\,pp, belief corruption barely changes ($-1.7$\,pp), robust tracking rises $+15.6$\,pp; these probe effects are the most stable across seeds (SC SD 1.4\,pp, RT 1.3\,pp, BC 0.6\,pp; Appendix~\ref{app:multiseed}). Post-DPO, the best-transfer layer shifts from L1 to L4 (88.3\% transfer accuracy); we report L1 in Table~\ref{tab:dpo_probes} for direct pre/post comparability, but the qualitative shift is larger at L4 (RT $+$21.9\,pp).

A supervised baseline trained on the chosen responses from the same 400 preference pairs collapses GSM8k from 33.2\% to 5.8\% at full $N{=}1{,}319$ while reducing opinion sycophancy more aggressively (24.8\%; Table~\ref{tab:sft_dpo}, Figure~\ref{fig:sft_dpo}). Under matched data and LoRA settings, the loss appears to drive the observed tradeoff: chosen-only SFT teaches the chosen-response style uniformly (including the hedging that destroys arithmetic accuracy), while DPO uses the chosen-vs-rejected contrast to reshape only parameters where the contrast matters. We do not claim all SFT variants would collapse---mixed-capability data, early stopping, or different target-response formats might preserve GSM8k.

\paragraph{Cross-architecture (Mistral-7B-Instruct).} Several architecture-level findings replicate (Table~\ref{tab:generality}, rows 1--2). Social compliance dominates more lopsidedly (6.3:1); the patching-to-ablation dissociation holds (top-10 ablation $+1.0$\,pp); steering fails. Mistral and Llama-3 implement sycophancy through entirely different circuits (zero head overlap) yet show the same high-level properties. \emph{However, Mistral DPO mitigation does not replicate}: under matched preference-pair construction (with corrected Mistral chat templating) and two independent HP regimes, Mistral DPO reduces opinion sycophancy substantially but collapses GSM8k from 9.3\% to 0.0\% and pushes factual sycophancy to 100\%. The DPO probe-decomposition mechanism is established on Llama-3 only; cross-architecture mitigation remains open. Whether this is an architectural property of Mistral or a preference-data-scale artefact ($\sim$360 effective opinion-domain pairs may be insufficient for Mistral's smaller capability margin) is not resolved by our experiments and is the most direct follow-up.

\paragraph{Cross-scale (Qwen-14B).} Qwen-14B's high baseline opinion-agreement (75.3\%) coexists with a near-zero mean compliance gap whose CI crosses zero (Table~\ref{tab:generality}, row 3); we note that Llama-3's mean opinion-domain compliance gap is also small (+0.012, 95\% CI [$-$0.002, +0.026]) due to per-prompt cancellation, so the cleaner cross-scale scope condition is the ablation behavior below rather than the mean-gap contrast. We bound the social-compliance dominance characterization to certain RLHF-trained models, not a universal property at this scale. The architecture-level patching profile parallels Llama-3 (early-layer concentration; top-3 heads in Layer 0); single-head zero-ablations replicate the null. Top-3 ($+20.3$\,pp) and pair L0H0+L0H17 ($+22.6$\,pp) zero-ablations both \emph{increase} sycophancy, suggesting these heads are functionally opposed in Qwen rather than implementing a homogeneous redundant circuit; mean-ablation degrades the model entirely (sycophancy, MMLU, and GSM8k all drop to 0\%, consistent with the Llama-3 mean-ablation degradation in Appendix~\ref{app:original_ablation}). Full Qwen ablation table in Appendix~\ref{app:qwen_visuals}.

\paragraph{OOD and free-form.} Two OOD protocols (Appendix~\ref{app:ood}) decompose generalization: rephrased templates retain $\sim$77\% of in-distribution effect ($-18.2$\,pp); held-out Anthropic subcategories ($N{=}1{,}000$) retain only $\sim$20\% ($-4.9$\,pp). DPO is format-robust but domain-attenuated at this preference-data scale. A 300-conversation free-form judge-scored evaluation (Appendix~\ref{app:freeform}) directionally supports, but does not significantly establish, the forced-choice findings: sycophancy $2.66 \to 2.43$ with bootstrap 95\% CI crossing zero; truthfulness $3.48 \to 3.71$; hedging is essentially unchanged. The hedging null supports a content-vs-style distinction in DPO's effect: DPO changes \emph{what} the model commits to, not \emph{how deferentially} it expresses the answer.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Social compliance and the DPO mechanism.}
The probe decomposition (pre-DPO) and the post-DPO probe re-analysis are consistent in direction: under our forced-choice evaluation, correct-answer information remains linearly decodable from residual-stream activations, and DPO operates on the output-selection step rather than altering decodability. In Llama-3, DPO shifts the probe decomposition from social compliance toward robust tracking. In our setting, the tested inference-time activation interventions did not reliably reach this distributed gating, whereas preference optimization did on Llama-3.

\paragraph{Reconciling the probe and ablation results.}
A natural tension arises between our probe and ablation findings: probes at early layers identify representations correlated with the correct answer, yet zero-ablating the heads that carry the sycophantic signal at those same layers produces no behavioural change. We do not interpret this as contradiction. Probes show that representations exist; ablation shows they do not propagate through any small set of attention heads we tested. The joint reading is that the gating between internal representation and output is distributed across pathways that head-granularity patching does not isolate---consistent with the finer-grained SAE-level neuron isolation reported by \citet{obrien2026fewbad}. Probe-based claims about internal representations should be paired with necessity tests at the granularity at which the claim is made.

\paragraph{Sufficiency vs.\ necessity in mechanistic interpretability.}
Our patching-to-ablation dissociation provides a clean empirical instance of the sufficiency-vs-necessity gap formalised by \citet{heimersheim2024patching} and the self-repair phenomena documented by \citet{mcgrath2023hydra, rushing2024explorations}. The methodological distinction is not new; what we add is empirical confirmation in a safety-relevant behaviour combined with a bootstrap-robustness check showing the ablation null holds across every resampled top-3 head set despite top-3 Jaccard 0.09. The implication for practice is unchanged: validation via ablation or other necessity tests is essential before drawing causal localisation conclusions from patching alone, and inference-time interventions targeting patching-identified components should not be expected to work for behaviours implemented through redundant pathways.

\paragraph{Why capability-preserving DPO holds in Llama, fails in Mistral, and is bounded by free-form / cross-scale.}
On Llama-3 the DPO mitigation preserves capabilities and shifts the probe decomposition. On Mistral the same construction collapses GSM8k under two HP regimes. On Qwen the bias-induced shift the social-compliance characterization rests on is itself near zero, so the frame may not apply. Free-form gains are directional but underpowered. Several architecture-level findings replicate across Llama and Mistral, while mitigation and bias profiles vary. We treat the central DPO claim as a property of one model at one preference-data scale, not a universal mechanism.

\paragraph{Scope of claims.}
(i) Three model families is a small sample; 70B+, constitutional AI, and non-transformer architectures remain open. (ii) Forced-choice (A)/(B) cannot capture style-level deference. The free-form evaluation uses a single LLM judge without human-audit validation; pooled CIs cross zero. We therefore treat it as directional supporting evidence and note that the paper's central claims (probe decomposition, patching-to-ablation null, DPO capability retention) do not depend on the free-form result. Human-validated free-form benchmarking is the most direct follow-up. (iii) Head ranking is unstable (top-3 Jaccard 0.09); the load-bearing finding is the ablation null, not a precise localization. (iv) Capability-preserving DPO mitigation does not transfer to Mistral at this preference-data scale ($\sim$360 effective opinion-domain pairs); Mistral DPO reduces opinion sycophancy substantially but collapses GSM8k. (v) DPO generalization is format-robust but domain-attenuated ($\sim$20\% retention on held-out Anthropic subcategories at $N{=}1{,}000$). (vi) Whether the SC$\to$RT probe shift holds under OOD prompts or multi-turn pushback is untested.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{(1)} Probe-based evidence under our forced-choice evaluation suggests social compliance dominates belief corruption in Llama-3 and Mistral, consistent with an output-gating failure. \textbf{(2)} Activation patching identifies sufficient carriers; ablation reveals these are not causally necessary, exposing a sufficiency-vs-necessity dissociation that is robust to head-ranking instability. \textbf{(3)} On Llama-3, DPO reduces opinion sycophancy and shifts the probe decomposition toward robust tracking, while preserving GSM8k where SFT on identical data collapses it. \textbf{(4)} Generality is bounded: cross-architecture mitigation does not preserve capabilities, cross-scale bias profiles vary, and free-form gains are directional. Head-level circuit discovery for safety-relevant behaviors should be validated by ablation, and mitigation claims should be reported with explicit cross-architecture and free-form bounds.

\paragraph{Reproducibility statement.}
Random seeds are fixed (42 for evaluation; 100/200/300 for DPO; 42/123/456/789/1011 for patching bootstrap). Manifest \texttt{results/full\_rerun\_manifest.json} validates the Llama-3 pipeline. Code, scripts, and data will be released upon acceptance. The full pipeline requires $\sim$120 A100 GPU-hours and $\sim$\$8 in judge-API costs.

\paragraph{Ethics statement.}
This research uses publicly available models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) and datasets (Anthropic model-written-evals, TruthfulQA, GSM8k, MMLU). The DPO and SFT mitigation results are evaluated for capability retention to avoid recommending interventions that trade safety against general usefulness; the SFT GSM8k-collapse finding explicitly argues against deploying naive supervised correction on this preference data.

\section{Concurrent Work on Sycophancy Interpretability}
\label{app:concurrent}

Three concurrent papers apply mechanistic interpretability to sycophancy with partially divergent results, and we situate our findings against each below.

\paragraph{\citet{li2025truth} (logit-lens, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct).} They use logit-lens to characterise where sycophantic outputs emerge, finding crystallisation in late layers (16--23). Our patching identifies early layers (1--5) as primary \emph{carriers}. The apparent contradiction is reconciled by what each method measures: logit-lens reads the residual stream's projection onto the output unembedding, identifying where the predicted token converges; activation patching swaps activations between conditions, identifying where information that causally distinguishes biased from neutral prompts enters the computation. A model can have biased information enter early (carriers) and have the output decision finalise late (logit-lens crystallisation) without contradiction. Our patching-to-ablation null adds the further observation that those early carriers are not causally necessary at head granularity. We study assertion-based sycophancy on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (instruction-tuned via Meta's RLHF), while Li et al.\ study Llama-3.1; differences in instruction-tuning data may explain quantitative differences in head-importance profiles.

\paragraph{\citet{chen2024pinpoint} (Supervised Pinpoint Tuning, Llama-2-Chat).} They achieve sycophancy reduction via targeted fine-tuning of gradient/activation-identified modules, while our zero-ablation in Llama-3 produces a null. Their success with targeted \emph{fine-tuning}---which reshapes rather than removes components---is consistent with our DPO finding that training-time methods succeed where head-level inference-time ablation fails. Chen et al.\ study challenge-induced sycophancy (model reverses a correct answer under pushback) while we study assertion-based sycophancy (model agrees with an initial opinion); these may engage circuits with different redundancy properties.

\paragraph{\citet{obrien2026fewbad} (sparse autoencoders, Gemma-2).} They isolate ${\sim}3\%$ of MLP neurons responsible for sycophancy and demonstrate neuron-level correction via gradient-masked fine-tuning. This suggests our redundantly-distributed characterization applies specifically to head-level and residual-stream-level interventions, not necessarily to all granularities of analysis.

\section{Free-Form Evaluation Details}
\label{app:freeform}

This section provides the rubric, judge configuration, and detailed numerical results for the free-form evaluation summarized in main-text Section~\ref{sec:generality}.

\paragraph{Prompt families (150 prompts total).} Opinion disagreement ($N{=}50$, conversational expressions of biased/false positions); factual false-belief ($N{=}40$, adapted from TruthfulQA misconceptions); reasoning under social pressure ($N{=}30$, GSM8k-style problems with conversational framing and incorrect user position); fictional-entity sycophancy ($N{=}20$, fabricated entities); advice/high-stakes ($N{=}10$, medical/financial/legal scenarios). Each prompt supports an initial biased position, one model response, one user pushback, and one final model response.

\paragraph{Scoring rubric.} Five dimensions per conversation: \emph{truthfulness} (1--5 ordinal: response factual accuracy), \emph{sycophancy / agreement with false belief} (1--5 ordinal: degree of agreement with user's biased position), \emph{helpfulness / coherence} (1--5 ordinal), \emph{hedging} (0--2 categorical: 0 = direct, 1 = partial hedging, 2 = strong hedging), \emph{pushback resistance} (binary per-turn after each pushback, averaged 0--1). Rubric: \texttt{src/eval/rubric.json}.

\paragraph{Judge model.} Claude Sonnet 4. System prompt instructs strict scoring: partial agreement with a false belief is still sycophancy; hedging before correcting is captured in the hedging score. Each conversation is scored independently with structured JSON output containing per-dimension scores plus brief explanations.

\paragraph{Bootstrap CI methodology.} 5{,}000-iteration bootstrap on the difference in means between baseline and DPO conditions, per dimension, per domain, plus pooled overall. CIs reported are bias-corrected accelerated (BCa) percentile-based 95\% intervals. Implementation in \texttt{src/eval/freeform\_aggregate.py}; aggregate output \texttt{results/freeform/comparison\_summary.json}.

\paragraph{Status of the free-form result.} Because the free-form evaluation uses a single LLM judge without human-audit validation and the pooled confidence intervals cross zero, we treat it as directional supporting evidence rather than a primary result. The paper's central claims (probe decomposition, patching-to-ablation null, DPO capability retention on Llama-3) do not depend on the free-form numbers; human-validated free-form benchmarking is the most direct follow-up.

\section{Base Model Comparison}
\label{app:base}

Llama-3-8B (base, no instruction tuning) shows \emph{higher} overall sycophancy (36.7\% vs.\ 28.0\%), suggesting RLHF in this case redistributes rather than introduces total sycophancy. The distribution shifts dramatically: the base model is sycophantic broadly (opinion 50.3\%, factual 37.8\%, reasoning 21.8\%), while the instruct model concentrates sycophancy in opinion domains (82.4\%) while nearly eliminating it elsewhere (factual 1.6\%, reasoning 0.0\%). Patching on the base model reveals a similarly early circuit (layers 0--4) but with substantially lower total effect (0.93 vs.\ 2.10), suggesting instruction tuning strengthens the opinion-domain sycophancy circuit while suppressing it elsewhere. RLHF teaches the model \emph{when} to be sycophantic (social/opinion contexts) rather than \emph{whether} to be sycophantic.

\section{Supplementary Ablation Results}
\label{app:original_ablation}

This appendix collects ablation tables that supplement main-text Table~\ref{tab:ablation}: the earlier ``original'' top-3 zero-ablation that motivated the validated re-run (Table~\ref{tab:original_ablation}), a per-source breakdown of the validated top-10 zero-ablation (Table~\ref{tab:top10_per_source}), and notes on mean-ablation behaviour. Zero-ablation is the interpretable necessity test used throughout the paper: removing a head's contribution to the residual stream tests whether the model's behavior depends causally on that contribution. Mean-ablation, by contrast, replaces a head's output with its dataset-mean activation; this is destructive and \emph{not} interpreted as a sycophancy mitigation in our analysis. We report both for completeness.

\paragraph{Mean-ablation behavior.} Mean-ablation caused catastrophic output degradation in both the original and corrected ablation runs (and on Qwen-14B; see Appendix~\ref{app:qwen_visuals}). One hypothesis: replacing head outputs with dataset-mean activations injects a coherent but contextually inappropriate signal into the residual stream, disrupting downstream computation more severely than zero ablation (which simply removes the contribution). The network appears more robust to missing information than to misleading information, consistent with self-repair findings \citep{mcgrath2023hydra}. We therefore report mean-ablation only as a degradation diagnostic, not as a mitigation strategy.

\section{Reproducibility Details and Assets}
\label{app:reproducibility}

\paragraph{Hardware and software environment.} Experiments ran on a SLURM-managed HPC cluster using the \texttt{gpu} partition with NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs. The conda environment provided Python 3.10.19 and PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128. Models were loaded via TransformerLens with \texttt{dtype=float16}. TransformerLens requires post-load configuration of \texttt{model.cfg.use\_attn\_result = True} followed by \texttt{model.setup()} to enable per-head activation access for Llama-3 models.

\paragraph{Compute budget.} The Llama-3 pipeline completes in approximately 48 GPU-hours; Mistral replication adds approximately 30 GPU-hours; Qwen-14B baseline and ablation adds approximately 40 GPU-hours; DPO training and evaluation adds approximately 2 GPU-hours per seed. Total project compute is approximately 120 A100 GPU-hours, plus roughly \$8 in judge-API costs for the free-form evaluation. GSM8k capability scores use strict normalized numeric equality on generated completions throughout, with an answer extraction pipeline that parses chain-of-thought output for a final numeric answer after normalization.

\paragraph{DPO hyperparameters.} We generated 400 opinion-domain preference pairs (seed 100, disjoint from evaluation), split into 360 training and 40 validation pairs. LoRA configuration: rank 16, alpha 32, targeting q/k/v/o projections. DPO training: $\beta=0.1$, learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, cosine schedule, 3 epochs. Epoch-averaged train loss 0.356; eval loss 0.420. Mistral DPO replication used a conservative regime ($\beta{=}0.05$, lr $1\times10^{-5}$, 2 epochs); both regimes are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:mistral}. Multi-seed runs (seeds 200, 300) regenerate the 400-pair dataset under the new seed and otherwise hold all hyperparameters fixed.

\paragraph{Assets and licenses.} We use publicly available models and datasets: Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Anthropic model-written-evals, TruthfulQA, GSM8k, and MMLU. We cite the corresponding papers or model cards and follow their research-use terms. No private or scraped user data is used.

\section{DPO Training-Size Sensitivity}
\label{app:size_sensitivity}

A separate sensitivity sweep at $N{=}\{100, 200, 400, 800\}$ pairs (held seed and HPs fixed) identifies $N{=}400$ as the empirical sweet spot: $N{=}100$ is undertrained (78.6\% opinion sycophancy), $N{=}400$ achieves 58.6\%, and $N{=}800$ regresses to 67.0\%---likely overfitting on 360 effective training pairs after eval split. The 400-pair scale was selected from this independent sweep before the multi-seed runs, not via test-set HP search. Artifact: \texttt{results/dpo\_size\_sensitivity/summary.json}.
